# The claim audit, batch 1: claims against evidence

Audited 14 September 2026. The numeric half is a committed artefact,
`data/processed/claim_inventory_2026.csv`, regenerated by
`scripts/inventory_claims.py` and coupled by recipe to the drafts and captions
so that a marker added or removed fails the suite until it is regenerated. This
file holds what a table cannot: the prose claims, the triage, and the
disagreements.

**Catalogued rather than fixed**, as the brief directed. The drafts are being
rewritten and correcting citations in prose that is about to change is wasted
work. Nothing in the drafts was edited by this pass.

---

## 1. The counts

1,262 numeric claims across four drafts and the caption file. (1,259 when this
section was written; marking the Hefei TCCON counts on 14 September 2026 moved
five numbers into `measured` and changed the row count.)

| category | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| measured — carries a resolver | **465** | 36.9 % |
| self-evident — structure, dates, configuration | 440 | 34.9 % |
| **neither — no resolver and no exemption** | **197** | **15.6 %** |
| cited — in a draft's own exemption list | 119 | 9.5 % |
| **unresolved — in an artefact, written without its marker** | 38 | 3.0 % |

**The fifth category is not in the brief and is the useful one.** The audit was
specified with four — measured, cited, neither, self-evident — and sampling the
classifications produced a fifth. A number that sits in a committed artefact and
carries no marker is **supported but not verified**: it is not an unsupported
claim and it is not a verified one. It is also the cheapest state to leave and
the easiest to mistake for either neighbour, and the remedy is one comment
rather than a measurement or a citation. Thirty-eight of them were found by
matching every unmarked number against every resolver's current value, which
means the artefact carries its own fix list.

**The introduction has no unsupported numbers at all**, which is the opposite of

introduction's own "Numbers not marked" note enumerates every literature figure

defects**, and the introduction has the first and not the second.

**The figure captions lead, and they were never in scope before.** 102 of the
197 unsupported numbers are in caption prose. The set-level consistency check
two passes ago covered the drafts and explicitly not the captions, and the
captions are where the drafts' discipline was not applied: they restate measured
quantities in their own words, at their own precision, without markers.

## 2. The prose claims, by class

The artefact is exhaustive for numbers and deliberately not for prose:
classifying "the sources are interspersed" needs a reading, not a rule. The
prose claims that matter are the ones asserting something external or something
about this domain, and they divide as below.

### 2a–2b. Claims needing a measurement, triaged

These are claims about this work or this domain that this work has not measured.
They are the more serious kind, because a paper asserting something about its own
domain without measuring it is making a claim it cannot support.

| claim | where | computable? |
|---|---|---|
| the sources are interspersed at the analysis cell's scale | introduction, discussion | **computable from committed data — now measured**, see §4 |
| the domain's sectoral composition | introduction (as a national prior) | **computable — now measured** on the analysis lattice, see §4 |
| no representativeness-error estimate exists for this composite | results §1 | **computable from committed data, and the statement is now false** — see §3 |
| the four provinces hold 26 percent of China's aquaculture area | introduction, discussion | **computable with data already on disk**: the national pond product gives 35.6 percent of national pond area inside the four provinces. Not run as a committed computation this pass |
| a held-out province's interior sits 150–200 km from the nearest training cell | was in results §5.3 | **computable, and already withdrawn** from the draft; the scratch figure is ~56 km median and is quoted nowhere because no script produces it |
| the coastline's coverage is worst where the largest landfill sits | grounding, caption | computable from committed data; not asserted in a draft |
| TROPOMI's ground pixel is 7 × 7 km | methods | **measured and in a granule attribute**, read in the Tier 3 pass; not yet in an artefact with a resolver |

### Claims needing a citation

Things this work read rather than did, asserted without naming a source. The
reference audit listed these by draft and they are not re-listed here; that list
is `notes/reference-audit.md` §2 and it remains the authority. The audit adds
one observation to it: **119 numeric claims are attributable from the drafts' own
exemption notes, and the notes are the only place the attribution exists.** A
reader of the paper sees twelve citations; a reader of the repository sees 119
attributed figures. The gap between those is the write-up's largest job.

### 2c. Claims supported by a citation where a local measurement would be stronger

**This is the most valuable category the audit can produce, and it does not
appear in the uncited counts because these claims are cited.** Four members.

**1. The interspersed-source claim, which rests on a US inversion.** The methods
and discussion both cite posterior error correlations from a US gridded
inventory — below 0.35 for a sector allocated on facility coordinates, 0.45 to
0.87 for sectors sharing a population surface — to establish that attribution
derives from the prior's spatial distinctness. That is borrowed evidence about a
different continent. **The same property is now measured in a Chinese inventory
over this domain**, in §4. The citation should stay as the mechanism's
precedent; the claim about *this* domain should be the measurement.

**2. The representativeness claim, which rests on Schutgens et al. (2017).**
Results §1 cites them for "observational coverage … is not an effective metric
to limit representation errors" and then says **"No representativeness-error
estimate was computed for this composite."** That sentence was true when written
and is now false: Tier 3 collected within-cell variance, and
`cell_quality_2018.csv` gives a **median within-cell standard deviation of
18.94 ppb against a between-cell spread of 14.86 ppb.** The scatter inside an
average cell exceeds the entire spatial signal between cells. **That is a
stronger statement than the citation, it is about this composite, and the draft
currently denies having it.**

**3. The Arabian Peninsula analogue.** The introduction and discussion both use a
downscaling study from another region — R² 0.63, RMSE 13.26 ppb — to argue that
methane is the hard case, and compare its RMSE to this field's 14.86 ppb spread.
The draft is honest that it is "a comparator from another region". **This work's
own numbers make the same argument locally**: its best land-cover model reaches
an RMSE of 14.21 ppb against that same 14.86 ppb spread, which is 95.7 percent
of the field's entire between-cell variation. The analogue can stay as
corroboration and should not be doing the work.

**4. The 26 percent aquaculture share.** A cited national statistic about this
region. The national pond product on disk measures **35.6 percent of national
pond area inside the four provinces** — a materially different number for a
related but not identical quantity, since the cited figure covers aquaculture
generally and the product covers artificial ponds specifically. **The paper
should either measure it or scope the citation to say what it counts.**

## 3. Consistency

### 3a. Inconsistent form

**No claim appears at two different values.** 107 resolvers are quoted in more
than one file and every one agrees.

**Sixteen are quoted at two precisions**, which extends the earlier set-level
finding from five to sixteen by adding the captions. The pattern is systematic
rather than random: **the captions round to fewer places than the drafts**.
`baseline.impervious_r2` is 0.0847 in a caption and 0.085 in the methods;
`baseline.observed_span_ppb` is 106 and 106.1; `baseline.impervious_span_ppb`
is 42.7 and 43. The claim checker verifies each at its own written precision, so
all 594 pass and none is an error. A copy-editor will still flag it, and the
right fix is one precision per quantity across the paper rather than per
section.

### 3b. Claims contradicting a record

**One substantive**: results §1's "No representativeness-error estimate was
computed for this composite", above.

**Two self-referential**, and they are worth recording because nothing can catch
them. `draft-introduction.md`'s note says "Six of this section's numbers carry
resolvers" — there are **12**. `draft-discussion.md`'s says "24 of this
section's numbers carry resolvers; 81 do not" — there are **28**. Both went
stale as later passes added markers, and **both are claims a draft makes about
itself that no mechanism verifies**: one is spelled as a word and the other is
an unmarked digit, so the claim checker cannot see either. The inventory artefact
now makes them checkable and they were left unfixed by instruction.

### 3c. Claims at the wrong strength

**One overstatement, and the new measurement is what exposes it.** The
introduction says the seven sectors "are all present and interleaved". The
measurement says four of the five inventoried sectors are heavily interleaved —
805 of 860 observed cells carry at least two sectors above a 5 percent share —
and **coal is nearly disjoint from the rest**: it shares one cell with rice and
five with landfills, occupying 22 cells while holding 39.6 percent of the
domain's inventoried emissions. "All present and interleaved" is true of
presence and false of interleaving for the largest sector.

*And the correction strengthens the argument rather than weakening it.* The
identifiability limit says a spatially distinct sector can be attributed and a
sector sharing an allocation surface cannot. **Coal is the distinct one**, which
is what the limit predicts, and it is also the sector neither predictor
represents. The precise claim is better than the blanket one.

## 4. The two measurements

`data/processed/sector_composition_2018.csv`, from
`scripts/measure_sector_composition.py`. **Inventory allocations, not
observations of sources** — the object the identifiability limit is about, and
not a measurement of where the sources are. Every row says so in its `basis`
column.

### The domain's sectoral composition on the analysis lattice

860 of 1,023 cells carry any inventory emission.

| sector | share of the domain | share of its national total | cells |
|---|---|---|---|
| coal | **39.59 %** | 13.18 % | **22** |
| rice | 29.50 % | 28.47 % | 798 |
| wastewater | 15.19 % | 29.43 % | 858 |
| landfills | 15.00 % | 16.21 % | 858 |
| oil and gas | 0.72 % | 0.71 % | 698 |

**The structural finding is the mismatch between mass and extent.** Coal is the
largest sector in the domain's inventoried budget and occupies **22 of 860
cells** — 2.6 percent of them. Rice is second by mass across 798. So the
domain's dominant inventoried sector is point-like and the second is diffuse,
which is a statement about attributability rather than about emissions: a
point-like sector is separable and a diffuse one competing with two other
diffuse sectors is not.

### The interleaving statistic, across thresholds rather than at one

A cell carries a sector when that sector holds at least the stated share of the
cell's total. **The threshold is swept because a threshold chosen to flatter the
claim is this measurement's failure mode.**

| threshold | cells with ≥2 sectors | ≥3 | ≥4 | median sectors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % | 848 | 797 | 296 | 3.0 |
| 2 % | 845 | 743 | 160 | 3.0 |
| 5 % | 805 | 557 | 38 | 3.0 |
| 10 % | 692 | 388 | 3 | 2.0 |
| 15 % | 592 | 294 | 1 | 2.0 |
| 20 % | 540 | 190 | 0 | 2.0 |
| 25 % | **488** | 72 | 0 | 2.0 |

**The claim survives the harshest threshold swept.** At 25 percent — where a
sector must contribute a quarter of a cell's emissions to count — 488 of 860
observed cells still carry two or more. So the interspersed-source claim does
not depend on a generous cut. What does depend on the cut is the *number* of
sectors: the median is three at 1–5 percent and two at 10 percent and above.

**And the pairwise table says which sectors are confounded**, at 5 percent:
landfills with wastewater 672 cells, rice with landfills 658, rice with
wastewater 559 — against **rice with coal in 1 cell** and coal with landfills in
5. The confounding is among the three population- and cropland-allocated
sectors; coal stands apart.

### What these change

**They change one draft sentence and strengthen two arguments.** The
introduction's "all present and interleaved" needs qualifying (§3c). The
identifiability argument gains a local measurement in place of a US citation
(§2c). And the coal claim gains a reason: coal is not merely unmodelled by both
predictors, it is the one sector distinct enough that an inversion could
attribute it, which makes its absence from the predictors a sharper omission.
